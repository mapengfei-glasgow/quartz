## A 4-Week Plan to Start Speaking

### Week 1: Listen and Shadow Alone

She should not try to speak with natives yet. First, train her mouth alone.

| Daily exercise | How to do it | Time |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Shadowing** | Play 30 seconds of a clear podcast or TV show (e.g., *6 Minute English*). Speak *along with* the speaker, 0.5 seconds behind. Do not pause. | 10 min |
| **Echoing** | Play one sentence. Pause. Repeat it exactly. Record herself. Compare. | 10 min |
| **Mouth warm-up** | Say difficult sounds repeatedly: "th" (this, that, these), "r" (red, right), vowel differences (ship/sheep). | 5 min |

**Goal by end of Week 1:** She can shadow for 1 minute without stopping.

### Week 2: Scripted Interactions

Now she practices *specific* real-life situations. Write down what she will say.

| Situation | Script to practice aloud |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ordering coffee | "Hi, can I have a latte to go, please?" |
| Buying a train ticket | "A single to [station], please. How much is that?" |
| Asking for directions | "Excuse me, is this the way to the museum?" |
| Checking out at a supermarket | "Sorry, do you take card?" |

She should practice each script **20 times aloud** before trying it in public.

**Goal by end of Week 2:** She can complete three real transactions without panic.

### Week 3: Safe, Short Conversations

She needs **controlled** conversations with predictable answers.

| Who to talk to | Why it is safe |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A shop cashier** | Conversation is short and predictable: "Hi. That's £4.50. Thank you. Bye." |
| **A bus driver** | Same questions every time: "Is this bus going to X?" |
| **A librarian** | Quiet environment. They are trained to be patient. |
| **An elderly neighbor** | Often lonely and happy to speak slowly. |

**What to say:** Prepare three questions she can ask anyone:
- "Excuse me, what time is it?"
- "Do you know where the nearest post office is?"
- "Sorry, could you say that again more slowly?"

**Goal by end of Week 3:** She has spoken to 5 different strangers for 30 seconds each.

### Week 4: Add 1-2 Minutes to Each Conversation

Now she extends slightly.

| Instead of just... | Add one more sentence |
| :--- | :--- |
| "A coffee, please." | "A coffee, please. It's cold today, isn't it?" |
| "Thank you." | "Thank you. Have a good day." |
| "Where is the bank?" | "Where is the bank? I'm new to this area." |

**Goal by end of Week 4:** She can have a 1–2 minute conversation without extreme anxiety.

## What NOT to Do

| Common mistake | Why to avoid it |
| :--- | :--- |
| Try to talk to anyone for a long time | She will freeze and feel worse. |
| Go to a pub or party | Fast, overlapping, drunk speech. Nightmare difficulty. |
| Apologize for her English | "Sorry for my bad English" makes her feel small and invites pity. Just speak. |
| Compare herself to others | There will be immigrants who speak better. Ignore them. Her journey is hers. |

## Practical UK-Specific Tips

| Tip | Why it helps |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Use "cheers" instead of "thank you"** | It sounds more natural and friendly. One word builds confidence. |
| **Say "sorry" for everything** | British people say sorry constantly. "Sorry, can I just squeeze past?" It is an easy, low-stakes word. |
| **Listen to BBC local radio** | Not national BBC. Local radio has slower speech, simpler topics, and phone-ins from normal people. |
| **Go to a "council" library** | Free, quiet, and librarians are paid to help. Ask for "Easy Reads" – books for adults learning English. |

## Week 1: Mouth Training (No real conversations yet)

**Goal:** Train her mouth muscles. Remove the fear of making sounds.

| Day | Activity | Time | Script to practice aloud (20x) |

## Week 4: Handle the Unexpected

**Goal:** Learn how to respond when she does NOT understand.

This is the most important skill for a B1 learner in the UK.

| Day | Problem | What to say (memorize) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Day 22** | She didn't hear | *"Sorry, could you say that again?"* |
| **Day 23** | She didn't understand a word | *"Sorry, what does [word] mean?"* |
| **Day 24** | The person spoke too fast | *"Sorry, could you speak more slowly, please?"* |
| **Day 25** | She needs time to think | *"Let me see…"* or *"Hmm, that's a good question…"* (These fillers buy 2-3 seconds) |
| **Day 26** | She made a mistake and knows it | *"Sorry, I mean…"* (Then correct herself. Native speakers do this all the time.) |

**Week 4 success check:** When she does not understand, she uses one of these phrases instead of freezing or walking away.
